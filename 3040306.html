Warm-Up

If the title and body text change wildly on every page, what might confuse a young reader?

Learn Together · BenQ

Look at the teacher's page examples and discuss what a young reader would notice.

  1. A children's book needs clear beginning, middle and ending. Check that pages follow your intended story order and all original story text is present.
  2. A readable publication has consistent body type, predictable margins, and a title that stands out from story text.
  3. Different layouts are allowed, but repeating a few clear design decisions makes the book feel like one finished project.

Whole-class checkpoint: Explain which change would help a reader and why before moving to computers.

Practice Together · Teacher demonstration

  1. On the BenQ compare two example interior slides: one with a tiny body font and clutter, another with a clear title, body and illustration area.
  2. Together list three precise edits, then model making them using font size, text-box width, and alignment.
  3. Practice moving from one page to the next, checking continuity and consistent readable text.

Try it aloud: Name the exact tool and the reader benefit of one modeled improvement.

Perform · Work on your actual storybook

  1. Reopen your book. Put the cover and story pages in their correct reading order.
  2. Check that the complete story is present and each page's words fit its text boxes. Split long passages over pages rather than shrinking them until unreadable.
  3. Apply consistent body-text size, intentional margins and readable contrast. Keep varied page layouts only when they improve comprehension.
  4. Review every illustration. If a picture covers text, resize, move or reorder it. Accept minor uneven whitespace rather than chasing perfect alignment.
  5. Save the editable version and check that you can reopen it.

Definition of finished: A readable complete book, intentional illustrations, editable source file and checked PDF when exporting is assigned. Do not sacrifice legibility for perfect art placement.
