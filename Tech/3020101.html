02 · Listen, talk, examine

Group Learn

Stay with the class. Watch the teacher improve a digital publication on the BenQ. Predict how each edit will help a young reader.

  1. Text boxes: Use Insert → Text box, keep paragraphs wide enough to read, and leave space around the edges.
  2. Pictures: Use teacher-approved artwork or a permitted screenshot (Windows + Shift + S), insert/paste it into Google Slides, and resize using a corner handle. Crop only when needed.
  3. Layers: If a picture covers words, move or resize it. When appropriate, use Arrange → Order so the picture stays behind the text.
  4. Page turns: Move to a new page at a natural change of scene or idea, not halfway through an important thought.
  5. Reader test: Ask where the eye starts and whether the pictures actually explain the story.

Say the words: illustration, text box, crop, layer, page break, audience, hierarchy.

Mastery Question 1

Explain how a digital publisher can capture an illustration, adjust its size, and arrange it with text to create a readable, effective publication.

Build Your Answer

Use one or more starters, or write your own explanation:

Extra Help

05 · Apply it

Perform

Make a new Unit 2 publication. You are not required to repair or continue last week’s Unit 4 storybook. Your goal today is a readable opening and a planned second page in Google Slides.

  1. Open Google Slides using the approved school launcher, then create a new presentation. Name it Unit 2 Storybook — Your First Name. Do not rename or overwrite your older project.
  2. On Page 1, add a short title and a readable text box. You may write your own original beginning or use this supplied practice text: “Mila found a folded map inside a library book. A tiny green arrow pointed toward the school garden. She wanted to know where it led.”
  3. Reserve an illustration space beside the words. If you have an approved artwork file, insert it, resize it by a corner handle, and ensure that no words are covered. If not, leave an outlined placeholder instead.
  4. Add a second page and decide where the story naturally turns. Add the next sentence or a brief note about what happens next. Keep the title and body text consistent.
  5. View both pages at normal zoom. Make at least one improvement for the reader, and confirm the file is saved in your school Drive.

A page turn should follow a meaningful moment, such as a new scene, action, or change of speaker. Keep a whole thought together so readers are not forced to guess what happened. This example is available only if your teacher activates extra independent support.
