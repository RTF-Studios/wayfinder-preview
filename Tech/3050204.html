Today's Mission

﻿TECH UNIT 5 · PART 2 · DAY 4 | Random Is Not Always Fair Student ______________________ Period ____ LEARNING TARGET — Collision sensing, conditional consequences, fairness, pacing and predictive difficulty.

TODAY'S MISSION — Analyze two hazard rules; state which could feel unfair and why.

TEKS — §126.18(c)(3)(B).

LEARN / WHY THIS MATTERS (8–15 min)

A condition IF touching player THEN applies a consequence, but only when sensing truly detects contact. Random spawn locations add unpredictability; however, sudden invisible hazards or an impossible speed make randomness unfair. A well-designed difficulty curve begins manageable, teaches feedback, then increases challenge with warnings. The player should understand why they lost.

REAL GAME STUDIO EXAMPLE

Bad design: every rock appears directly over the player's position without warning, and it falls instantly. Improved design: obstacle begins high on screen, falls at a readable speed, and moves to a new random x only after it leaves the stage; collision ends a life with visible feedback.

PERFORM — FOLLOW IN ORDER (25–32 min)

Analyze two hazard rules; state which could feel unfair and why. Propose two changes to spawns/speed/warning and one paired playtest to compare original and improved versions. On a paper diagram mark player hitbox, falling path, and what score/life changes after actual touch.

SELF-TEST / EXPECTED OUTCOME

The proposed rule can be tested with at least three play attempts; compare evidence, not just opinion.

EXTRA HELP

Start with the smallest version of the core game. Work on ONE script or rule at a time. Run the game after each change; compare expected and observed behavior. On paper, draw the blocks in sequence and describe the expected output. If software is unavailable at home, complete the algorithm or screen plan on paper rather than falsely claiming a live software test.
