Warm-Up

Pictures can change how readers feel about the exact same sentence. Imagine reading “Moss sat alone beside the empty swings.” One picture shows a quiet, empty playground. Another shows a busy party with cheerful friends. Both may look appealing, but only one clearly matches the story.

02 · Learn together

Group Learn — Illustrations and Page Turns

Illustrations show setting, action and mood. Choose art that fits the sentence and keep the words readable. A page turn should happen after a complete thought or at a meaningful new scene, not in the middle of a sentence just to fill space.

Scene fits

Moss sits by the empty swings. A quiet playground helps show the feeling of being left out.

Scene distracts

A cheerful crowded picnic sits beside words saying Moss feels alone.

Teacher demonstration: Choose approved art; press Windows + Shift + S to capture an illustration and Ctrl + V to paste into Slides. Resize using a corner. Move the art beside or behind the words without covering them.

Purposeful page turn

Page one ends: “Moss heard a frightened chirp.” Page two shows Moss finding the hatchling.

Awkward cut

Page one ends “Moss followed the…” and the next begins “…sound into the fog.”

Do not change the story's original wording. Your work is selecting, arranging and publishing it.

Moss and the Rumor

Use the author's original words, in their original order. You are the publisher, not the author.

At the edge of the foggy playground lived Moss, a shy green swamp monster. More than anything, Moss wanted someone to play with. One morning, a crow whispered, “Moss scares anyone who comes near.” Soon the other creatures moved away whenever Moss walked past. Moss sat alone beside the empty swings. Then a tiny hatchling wandered into the fog and could not find its family. Everyone heard its frightened chirps, but the mist hid the path. Moss knew the swamp trails by heart. “Follow my footsteps,” Moss said gently. Step by step, Moss led the hatchling home. The other creatures gathered around. “We believed a rumor instead of getting to know you,” one apologized. “Would you like to play?” Moss smiled. The next morning, the swings were no longer empty.
