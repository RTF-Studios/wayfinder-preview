Learn 1 — Give the Reader a Path

Words have different jobs. A title announces the story; body text carries the action. A larger title, comfortable body font and whitespace guide the eye. Huge pictures or squeezed paragraphs can interrupt the story even when every word is correct.

Look closely: The page design must work for ages 9–11 at normal zoom. Good layouts can differ; the reader should always know where to begin.

Use approved art: Choose your own or teacher-supplied illustrations. Taking a screenshot does not give permission to use somebody else's picture. For approved art, press Windows + Shift + S, select the illustration, then use Ctrl + V in Slides. Drag a corner handle to resize without stretching; use Arrange → Order if you need to change layers.

Teacher-led Practice — Fix the Crowded Page

  1. In Google Slides select a crowded text box by its border, not the blinking text cursor.
  2. Widen or move the box. Make a separate, larger heading only if the page needs one.
  3. Select the illustration and drag a corner handle to resize without stretching.
  4. Place the image away from sentences; if needed choose Arrange → Order to manage layers. Read the page at normal zoom.

Predict what the reader will notice first before each change.

Original story · supplied reading resource

Moss and the Rumor

Use the author's original words, in their original order. You are the publisher, not the author.

At the edge of the foggy playground lived Moss, a shy green swamp monster. More than anything, Moss wanted someone to play with. One morning, a crow whispered, “Moss scares anyone who comes near.” Soon the other creatures moved away whenever Moss walked past. Moss sat alone beside the empty swings. Then a tiny hatchling wandered into the fog and could not find its family. Everyone heard its frightened chirps, but the mist hid the path. Moss knew the swamp trails by heart. “Follow my footsteps,” Moss said gently. Step by step, Moss led the hatchling home. The other creatures gathered around. “We believed a rumor instead of getting to know you,” one apologized. “Would you like to play?” Moss smiled. The next morning, the swings were no longer empty.

  4. Follow the reading order at normal zoom. Improve whitespace, alignment or the illustration's placement.
  5. Save both improved pages and be ready to explain one decision.

Learn 2 — When Should a Page Turn?

A reader understands a story more easily when connected words and actions stay together. A scene change or new decision can create a purposeful pause.
