Make Your Own Falling-Objects Game

Explore the game. Change its world. Then build Level 2.

Our three goals

Concept: A game follows rules and gets harder as you play. Skill: I can change art and coding blocks in TurboWarp. What did I create? My own two-level falling-objects game.

Open the downloaded project in the installed TurboWarp editor: File → Load from your computer. Do not start a blank project. Save your own copy.

Teacher challenge — play Mr. G’s version!

Want to see how far a falling-objects game can go? Download Mr. G’s game, load it in TurboWarp, and playtest it. Look for interesting gameplay ideas you could adapt in your OWN project. This is a separate example; keep your starter and your own saved game.

In TurboWarp: File → Load from your computer. Open this download to play Mr. G’s game.

Thursday · Level 1

Make the game yours

Play the starter for one minute. Move with the left and right arrow keys. Collect the falling shapes to score points. Missing them costs a life.

  1. Open the starter and test that the player moves and objects fall.
  2. Pick a theme for your world: space, ocean, candy, sports, fantasy—your choice.
  3. Use TurboWarp's Costumes tab to redraw the Player sprite.
  4. Redraw the costumes for Falling 1, Falling 2, and Falling 3. Make three different objects.
  5. Click Stage → Backdrops and make a matching background.
  6. Press the green flag. Make sure it still works. Save: File → Save to your computer.
Thursday finish line: one original player, three custom falling objects, one original background, and a game that still runs.
Friday · Level 2

Make the game harder

Difficulty ramp means the game changes after the player makes progress. Your Level 2 only needs ONE clear difference.

  1. Open the game you saved Thursday.
  2. Playtest with a partner. Ask: “Is Level 1 too easy, too hard, or just right?”
  3. Click the Player sprite and find its green-flag script.
  4. Inside its forever loop, add this simple rule:

ifScore > 9thenset Speed to 6

  1. Run the game. When the score reaches 10, the falling objects should speed up. That's Level 2!
  2. Add one extra fun detail if time allows: new colors, a bonus object, a sound, or a “Level 2!” message.
  3. Let another student test it. Ask what feels different. Fix one thing and save the final game.
Friday finish line: Level 1 works, Level 2 gets harder, and a partner tested your game.

Quick help

  • The game won't move? Click the green flag. Use the arrow keys.
  • Where is Speed? Look under Variables; it is already in the starter.
  • Can't find a block? Ask your teammate first, then ask Mr. G.
  • Game got stuck? Green flag restarts Score, Lives, and Speed.
