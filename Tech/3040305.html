Learn Together · Books bring words and pictures together

When your teacher invites you: Move your chair to the Learn Together classroom position. Leave your individual work at your assigned seat.

A storybook illustration can sit beside words, behind them, or even overlap some letters. The test is simple: can readers still understand the story?

The Cat in the Hat — Dr. Seuss. Notice how the hat crosses in front of the title without hiding the words. The picture and title feel like one design.

Marlow — written by Ross Neild, illustrated by Andy Catling.

On the published cover, the pirate mouse stands below the large title. The clear turquoise sky gives the letters room to breathe. There is still plenty of action!

Teacher can display the supplied Marlow cover alongside this example.

Talk together: Where do your eyes go first? How do text and art share space? Can overlapping art ever help a design? Yes—if the words remain readable.

Story behind the page: Real children's-book designers plan pictures and letters as a team. They use empty space and overlapping layers to guide the reader's eyes—like actors sharing a stage.

Perform · Add a picture to a page you already made

  1. Open your storybook in Google Slides.
  2. Catch up if needed: Your slide should already have two sentences and room for a picture. If it doesn't, finish that text layout first.
  3. Choose and paste a picture from the library above (or a teacher-approved picture).
  4. Resize or move it so readers can still see every word. Keep your slide saved.

Finished for today: At least one existing story page has an illustration, and its words remain readable. You do not need a completed book or exported PDF today.

Creative Labs · Three assessed challenges

All three are required. Use the same storybook and tools you already know.

Creative Lab 1 · Placement alternatives

Compare picture above, below, or beside one paragraph; describe which is easiest to read.

Worked example: Try: illustration to the right vs. below the same paragraph. Keep the words unchanged; compare reading order.

Creative Lab 2 · Story meaning

Explain how an illustration helps readers understand one exact moment in your story.

Worked example: Choose a story moment (character, setting, action) and explain what the picture reveals that words alone do not.

Creative Lab 3 · Overlap repair

Describe one text/picture collision and the exact tool you used to fix it.

Worked example: If an image hides the last line, try moving or resizing it; document the control and the repaired result.
