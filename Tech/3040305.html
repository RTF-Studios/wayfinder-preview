Warm-Up

What is harder to read: a page with no picture, or a page where a picture covers the words? Why?

Learn Together · BenQ

Look at the teacher's page examples and discuss what a young reader would notice.

  1. Illustration should help readers understand the action, character or feeling—not hide the story.
  2. Use Win + Shift + S to capture a permitted picture when needed, then paste into Google Slides. Crop unwanted edges using Crop; drag a corner to resize without stretching.
  3. Arrange the picture beside the story. If text must sit on it, use Arrange → Order to move the picture behind text when supported. White background or extra whitespace can be acceptable.

Whole-class checkpoint: Explain which change would help a reader and why before moving to computers.

Practice Together · Teacher demonstration

  1. Project two sample pages: one with story words blocked by art, another with the words readable beside the art. Ask which can be read in five seconds.
  2. Demonstrate inserting one illustration on a copied example slide, resizing by a corner, and placing it away from the words.
  3. Show a deliberate overlap, then repair it together. Check the result from normal reading size.

Try it aloud: Name the exact tool and the reader benefit of one modeled improvement.

Perform · Work on your actual storybook

  1. Open your current children's storybook in Google Slides and identify the pages that would benefit from an illustration.
  2. Insert an original or teacher-approved illustration where appropriate. Use Screenshot (Win + Shift + S) and paste if that is your approved image source.
  3. Resize and position the art without covering words. Arrange layers only if needed, and leave sufficient blank space for easy reading.
  4. Read every newly illustrated page at normal zoom. Fix any overlap, tiny type or distracting crop; save the original editable file.
  5. Make a note of any page still needing illustration or final formatting for tomorrow.

Definition of finished: A readable complete book, intentional illustrations, editable source file and checked PDF when exporting is assigned. Do not sacrifice legibility for perfect art placement.

Creative Labs · Three assessed challenges

All three are required. Use the same storybook and tools you already know.

Creative Lab 1 · Placement alternatives

Compare picture above, below, or beside one paragraph; describe which is easiest to read.

Worked example: Try: illustration to the right vs. below the same paragraph. Keep the words unchanged; compare reading order.

Creative Lab 2 · Story meaning

Explain how an illustration helps readers understand one exact moment in your story.

Worked example: Choose a story moment (character, setting, action) and explain what the picture reveals that words alone do not.

Creative Lab 3 · Overlap repair

Describe one text/picture collision and the exact tool you used to fix it.

Worked example: If an image hides the last line, try moving or resizing it; document the control and the repaired result.
