Warm-Up · Why Pages Look Different

Printed books were designed around paper, binding, and the reader's needs. A novel often uses one steady reading path. A magazine may offer several short sections to explore. A children's story often leaves more space for pictures and uses fewer words at a time. Different purposes lead to different layouts.

Learn 1 · The Traditional Reading Page

A margin is the open space around a page. Line length is how far each line stretches before it wraps. Alignment is how text lines up. A continuous story needs an obvious reading order.

A tiny text box causes words to wrap after only a few words. A text box too close to the slide edge can look crowded or hide text. Wider text boxes, consistent alignment, and reasonable margins improve readability.

Original Story Library

Perform 1 · Page One: A Calm Reading Path

  1. Add a blank interior slide directly after your cover, keeping Slide 1 unchanged.
    1. Use Slide → New slide, then choose a Blank layout if necessary.
  2. Copy the opening passage of your selected story from the library above. Keep the words in order.
  3. Insert one main text box and paste the passage into it.
    1. Choose Insert → Text box, drag the box, click inside, then paste.
  4. Adjust the font, size, alignment, and box width to create a traditional, single reading path.
  5. Check all words are visible, the margins feel comfortable, and the story reads naturally. Minor imperfect gaps are fine.

Learn 2 · Magazine and Website Thinking

Magazines developed columns, headlines, and side notes to organize different kinds of information on printed pages. Websites borrowed some of these ideas but also make room for scanning, scrolling, and screens of different sizes.

Hierarchy means what readers notice first. Proximity keeps related material together. Whitespace provides visual breathing room. Children's books can use those ideas without making the story hard to follow.

Practice 2 · A Different Arrangement (1–2 minutes)

Compare one wide paragraph with two aligned smaller blocks. Identify where a reader would begin, then where the reader would look next. No troubleshooting or extra slide required.

Perform 2 · Page Two: A Contrasting Layout

  1. Add a second interior slide immediately after Page One and select the next consecutive passage from your story. Do not repeat or skip words.
  2. Make a visibly different arrangement: two aligned text areas or an offset text block with whitespace reserved for a future illustration.
    1. Two boxes are fine as long as readers can follow the paragraphs in order.
  3. Create hierarchy through sensible placement, spacing, or modest differences in text size.
  4. Keep margins, line wrapping, and alignment readable. Do not insert illustrations today.
  5. Compare the two pages. The layouts must genuinely differ, but both should be comfortable to read.
