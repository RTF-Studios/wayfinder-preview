Autumn Vector Art Studio

Choose a scene. Rebuild it with simple shapes. Add something of your own.

What am I learning, doing, and creating?

Concept

A raster picture has textures and tiny details. Vector art uses shapes, fills, outlines, and curves. Artists simplify what they see.

Skill

I can draw a background and separate sprites using TurboWarp's vector editor, including shapes and Bezier curves.

What did I create?

A new vector scene with one Stage backdrop, two sprites copied from the reference, and one original sprite I invented.

Choose from four reference scenes

On Thursday, pick one. On Friday, pick a different one. You do not need to copy every leaf, shadow, or texture. Look for the biggest shapes.

2 · Lakeside Sunset

Background: lake, cabin, mountains, sky. Sprite ideas: canoe, big maple leaf.

3 · Harvest Market

Background: market stand, hills, sky. Sprite ideas: pumpkin, basket of apples.

4 · Covered Bridge

Background: bridge, path, hills, sky. Sprite ideas: squirrel, large leaf.

Today's art challenge — 45 minutes

  1. 2 minutes: Choose your reference and decide which two objects will become sprites.
  2. 15 minutes: In the installed TurboWarp, use Stage → Backdrops → Paint. Make a simplified version of the background with big shapes and colors.
  3. 8 minutes: Add Sprite 1. Paint one foreground object from your chosen picture using vector tools.
  4. 8 minutes: Add Sprite 2. Paint a different foreground object from the reference.
  5. 7 minutes: Invent Sprite 3. Add something that belongs in your scene but isn't already there.
  6. 5 minutes: Let a partner see it, make one improvement, and File → Save to your computer.

Finish line for each day: 1 Stage backdrop + 2 reference-based sprites + 1 original sprite. Simple is good. Save a different project each day.

Artist's reminder: Use a few large shapes first. Ignore tiny textures. Put permanent scenery in the backdrop, and anything you might move or animate in its own sprite. Nothing needs to move today.

Partner feedback

Ask your partner: “What part of my scene do you recognize first? Which shape should I improve?” Give them one useful answer too.
