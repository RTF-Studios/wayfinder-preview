02 · New professional example

Group Learn — A Brand Becomes a Direction

Our Warm-Up asked what a passing viewer can notice. Now examine a different campaign and solve a different design problem: how can a billboard give useful directions without showing a whole restaurant or a full brand name?

New idea — recognition with very little information. The billboard uses familiar red and yellow, a cropped piece of the Golden Arches, and the short direction “on your right.” It does not need a photograph of the food. This is a wayfinding advertisement: its purpose is helping people find a place.

Why those choices? Hierarchy determines what you notice first; contrast helps the white direction remain readable; negative space keeps everything else out of the way; cropping allows only part of the famous logo to stand for the whole brand. A designer chooses each because the billboard must work quickly.

Don't confuse persuasion methods. Bandwagon means claiming that many others are doing something. This billboard gives directions; it does not make a bandwagon claim. In your iCEV work, the client requested bandwagon, so your message must actually use the required method instead of assuming any striking image counts.

Question to discuss: Why might showing less brand information make this billboard more useful for its audience?

03 · New situation, different reasoning

Group Practice — The Library After-Dark Event

Imagine a library's outdoor advertisement for a nighttime reading festival. Its headline is enormous, but the date is almost invisible and the event name blends into a dark photograph. This is a new client problem: essential event information is hard to find.

  1. Identify what a family must learn before deciding to attend.
  2. Choose one visible design change to prioritize the missing information without sacrificing the mood of the photograph.
  3. Design a three-second viewer test: what should a stranger be able to tell you after seeing the revised billboard?
Practice insight: A recognizable brand, a useful direction and a readable event date serve different purposes. Designers should not reuse one solution for every communication problem.

Idea 1

Headline Variant

Make one alternate headline for your existing billboard in Photopea. Compare which version a passing viewer could understand faster.

For example, reduce extra words while keeping the same accurate product message.
Idea 2

Contrast Experiment

Try changing the contrast between your main message and its background. Compare which version stays readable from farther away.

For example, a dark headline on a light area may be easier to read than two similar tones.
Idea 3

Viewer Test

Show your existing billboard briefly to a partner and ask what they noticed first. Record one insight that could improve your intended message.

For example, if they notice decoration before the product, reconsider which detail is largest.
