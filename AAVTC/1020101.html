Warm-Up

Think of an advertisement that suggests many people are using a product. How does it try to affect your decision?

Learn · A quick design insight

Bandwagon persuasion invites the audience to join a popular choice. Avoid unsupported claims such as invented sales figures. Your billboard must make the persuasion recognizable.

iCEV source: Communication Styles — Billboard Design. This project must use bandwagon persuasion and identify its information source, message, and audience/receiver.

Perform · Build the billboard

  1. Step 1. Choose one product from iCEV's electronics, retail, household, appliances, or furniture categories. Write its name at the top of a scratch plan.
  2. Step 2. Write the information source, message being conveyed, and audience/receiver. Decide what you want the audience to do after seeing the billboard.
  3. Step 3. Open Photopea. Choose File → New; set width 2000 px and height 1000 px. Create a landscape document. Name the project Billboard_Firstname_Lastname.
  4. Step 4. Sketch a headline, dominant product image or original illustration, and a short call to action. Use a visible bandwagon idea without fabricating popularity statistics.
  5. Step 5. Save your editable work using File → Save as PSD. Confirm the .psd file exists and can be reopened. Keep working on the same file tomorrow.

Production note: A strong billboard communicates at a glance. Prioritize readable words, an obvious visual focal point, and a specific audience over decoration.

iCEV rubric: Research & Organization 35; Concept & Understanding 35; Creativity/Craftsmanship 15; Production/Effort 15 (100 total).

Creative Labs · Three required challenges

All three are graded evidence tasks. Use only skills already practiced for this billboard.

Creative Lab 1 · Headline test

Draft two alternative bandwagon headlines using different tone; choose the clearer one and explain why.

Model: Compare two short headlines for the same product; choose the easier-to-read version.

Creative Lab 2 · Audience lens

Write how your billboard message changes for two realistic audiences; select one and justify.

Model: Define the audience and what they should do next; avoid unsupported statistics.

Creative Lab 3 · Visual map

Describe or sketch the hierarchy: first thing seen, second thing seen, final action. Explain the order.

Model: Plan a headline, product image and action in a reading order.
