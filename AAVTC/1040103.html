01 · Connect and notice · 4 minutes

Warm-Up

Before today's skill, think about a familiar shape. Think of a toy robot: would a head twice the size of the torso or half its size give the same impression? Why not?

02 · Study the concept and vocabulary

Group Learn · Build a Basic Character · Proportions

🎙️ Learning Leader: Read the following explanation and invite one student to describe what they notice.

A big head on a small body looks different from a small head on a wide torso. That difference is proportion. When a hand goes behind the torso, layer order hides part of it. Grouping keeps an entire set of parts together while you edit.

Today's design vocabulary

Proportion: The size of one character part compared with another.

Layer order: Which overlapping shape is drawn in front of another.

Grouping: Selecting several editable pieces so they move or scale together.

Professional design connection

Look at the simplified shapes in professional rounded robot characters such as Baymax. Visual proportions make a character recognizable even before facial details.

Teacher-supplied professional character references. Observe only the major geometric masses, not facial details.

03 · Teacher model

Group Practice · Watch, then try

Watch your teacher demonstrate the actual controls in the locally installed TurboWarp Vector Paint editor. The online editor is blocked at school.

  1. In installed TurboWarp, make a large torso oval or rectangle and smaller head oval; compare head-to-torso proportion.
  2. Create two arms and two legs from rectangles/ovals. Position one arm behind the torso, then bring it forward to show the difference.
  3. Rotate an arm upward to communicate a wave without drawing tiny details.
  4. Group the related pieces, move the assembled body, then ungroup to revise one arm.

05 · Create and save your editable work

Perform · Build a Basic Character · Proportions

Use rectangles and ovals only to build one original head, torso and limb character.

  1. Choose a simple character idea · Click to close or reopen help
    Choose an original human-like, animal or robot body. Start with a head and torso; do not trace an existing character.
  2. Build the six essential pieces · Click to expand extra help
    Create a head, torso, two arms and two legs as separately editable ovals or rectangles. No Reshape tool or freehand contours.
  3. Control proportions and pose · Click to expand extra help
    Use side/corner handles and rotation to change head/torso size and arm direction. Set intentional front/back order.
  4. Group for an organized edit · Click to expand extra help
    Group pieces to move the whole design, then ungroup one set for an improvement. Check a readable silhouette at small size.
  5. Save and submit · Click to expand extra help
    Save your native editable TurboWarp project and attach it to the correct Google Classroom assignment.
